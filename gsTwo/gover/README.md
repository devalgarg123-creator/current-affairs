2) Governance: Participatory Planning and Gram Sabha

Why is it in the news? : Special Gram Sabhas are being organised across India on 2 October 2026.

The Ministry of Panchayati Raj is also launching the People's Plan Campaign 2026–27, titled Sabki Yojana, Sabka Vikas.
The campaign aims to facilitate participatory and evidence-based preparation of Gram Panchayat Development Plans (GPDPs) for the financial year 2027–28.

Background: Gram Sabha
A Gram Sabha is a body consisting of persons registered in the electoral rolls of a village within a Panchayat's area.
Article 243A empowers the state legislature to provide for the powers and functions of Gram Sabhas.
The exact powers and responsibilities of Gram Sabhas vary according to state legislation.

The process can involve:
Identification of local infrastructure gaps.
Assessment of drinking water and sanitation needs.
Discussion of health and education services.
Identification of vulnerable households.
Prioritisation of development expenditure.
Convergence of government schemes.

Significance
A. Democratic decentralisation: Participatory planning allows local communities to influence development priorities rather than relying exclusively on top-down decisions.
B. Better targeting of public expenditure: Village-level planning can help identify local needs that may not be adequately reflected in state or national planning.
C. Social accountability: Gram Sabhas can provide a platform for citizens to question local representatives and review the implementation of development programmes.
D. Inclusive development: Participation by women, Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and disadvantaged communities can improve the representation of their needs.
E. Convergence of schemes: Local planning can help coordinate activities across different ministries and government departments.

Challenges
Limited participation: Gram Sabha meetings may not attract all sections of society
Elite capture: Influential groups may dominate local decisions
Financial constraints: Panchayats may lack adequate funds
Technical capacity: Preparation of evidence-based development plans requires trained personnel

Way forward
Ensure regular and accessible Gram Sabha meetings.
Encourage the participation of women and marginalised communities.
Strengthen the technical and financial capacity of Panchayats.
Use local data to identify development priorities.
Improve social audits and public disclosure of expenditure.
Link development plans with measurable outcomes.

Prelims facts

Part IX	Panchayats
Article 243A	Gram Sabha
Article 243G	Powers, authority and responsibilities of Panchayats
Eleventh Schedule	29 subjects that may be devolved to Panchayats

3) Special Intensive Revision (SIR), ECINET & Electoral Rolls

Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is a process undertaken by the Election Commission of India (ECI) to verify and revise electoral rolls so that eligible citizens are included and ineligible/dead/duplicate/shifted entries are removed.

The Election Commission's electoral-roll revision exercise remains a major governance issue. The ECI's citizen portal also provides facilities for the 2026 SIR, including enumeration forms, searching names in previous SIR rolls and submitting documents against notices.The Controversy behind SIR of electoral rolls with protests demanding the resignation of CEC. Critics have alleged irregularities and possible wrongful exclusion of voters, while the ECI maintains that the revision is intended to eliminate duplicate and ineligible entries. 

Article 324: Election Commission of India has the superintendence, direction and control of elections.
Article 325: one general electoral roll; no exclusion on grounds of religion, race, caste or sex.
Article 326: adult suffrage.
Representation of the People Act, 1950 → preparation and revision of electoral rolls.
Representation of the People Act, 1951 → conduct of elections and election-related offences.

Core issue:
There are two legitimate objectives that have to be balanced:
Objective 1: Remove ineligible/dead/duplicate/non-resident entries.
Objective 2: Ensure that genuine eligible citizens are not disenfranchised because of documentation or procedural difficulties.

Prevents duplicate entries.
Removes deceased voters.
Updates migration-related changes.
Improves electoral-roll accuracy.
Helps maintain confidence in elections.

Concerns
Wrongful deletion
Documentation burden
Migrant voters
Digital exclusion
Transparency
Accessibility of claims and objections
Eligible citizens may be wrongly excluded.
Migrants and vulnerable groups may face documentation difficulties.
Database errors and inadequate notice/hearing procedures may create wrongful deletions.

Balanced Mains analysis
Arguments supporting revision: Accurate electoral rolls reduce duplication, improve electoral administration and strengthen confidence in elections.
Concerns requiring safeguards: Inadequate documentation, inaccessible procedures, erroneous data matching or insufficient time for objections may disproportionately affect vulnerable groups and eligible voters.
The constitutional test is not simply whether the process removes errors; it is whether it does so lawfully, transparently and without arbitrary disenfranchisement.
Way forward
- Publish clear procedures and timelines.
- Provide accessible verification and correction mechanisms.
- Ensure that software flags trigger human scrutiny rather than automatic adverse decisions.
- Maintain transparent records of additions, corrections and deletions.
- Strengthen Independent grievance redressal.
Mains answer line:
"Electoral-roll revision must function as an instrument of electoral integrity, not become a procedural barrier to the exercise of universal adult franchise."

5) WHO Pandemic Agreement — India's role in global health governance
At the September 2026 UN High-Level Meeting on Pandemic Prevention, Preparedness and Response, countries reiterated the need for stronger global cooperation on future pandemics.
A major unresolved component is the Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing (PABS) system under the WHO Pandemic Agreement. World Health Organization
What is PABS?
Very simply:
Countries share pathogen samples/data → scientists develop vaccines/medicines/diagnostics → benefits should be shared equitably.
The central challenge is:
Why should developing countries provide pathogen samples if they may not receive affordable access to the resulting vaccines, medicines or diagnostics?

The WHO says the PABS annex is intended to operationalise timely sharing of pathogens and equitable sharing of benefits arising from their use. World Health Organization
India angle
India's position broadly combines:
- international cooperation
- national sovereignty
- equitable access
- affordable health products
- greater manufacturing capacity in developing countries
One Health connection
Pandemic preparedness increasingly follows the:
Human health + Animal health + Environmental health = One Health
framework. World Health Organization
