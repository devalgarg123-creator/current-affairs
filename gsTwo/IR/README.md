What is in the news?
India hosted the first BIMSTEC Home Ministers’ Meeting in New Delhi. The meeting adopted the Delhi Declaration, emphasising regional security and law-enforcement cooperation. 

IAS Prep



What is BIMSTEC?
BIMSTEC stands for the Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation.
Its seven members are:
- Bangladesh
- Bhutan
- India
- Myanmar
- Nepal
- Sri Lanka
- Thailand
It connects South Asia with Southeast Asia and provides a platform for regional cooperation.
Why does it matter for India?
1. Internal security: Cooperation against terrorism, radicalisation and terror financing.
2. Cybersecurity: Joint efforts against cybercrime, online fraud and misuse of digital technologies.
3. Maritime security: Cooperation in the Bay of Bengal, including responses to transnational threats.
4. Connectivity: Supports India's broader regional connectivity and Act East policy.
5. Strategic diversification: Offers an additional platform for regional engagement beyond other South Asian arrangements.
Challenges
- Political instability in parts of the region.
- Differences in institutional capacity and enforcement.
- Limited trade integration and connectivity bottlenecks.
- Cross-border crime and cyber threats that require trusted information-sharing.
Way forward
India should focus on intelligence-sharing protocols, joint capacity-building, maritime domain awareness, faster connectivity projects and consistent implementation of regional commitments.
Prelims revision: BIMSTEC has seven members; its geography centres on the Bay of Bengal region. Do not confuse it with SAARC or ASEAN.
