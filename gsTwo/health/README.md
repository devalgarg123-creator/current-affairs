What is in the news?
The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare has launched the JOSH (Joint Squad for Health) campaign across 11 states and 55 districts to mobilise young people, NCC cadets and local communities in the fight against tuberculosis.

Static background
Tuberculosis is an infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It primarily affects the lungs and spreads through the air.
India's policy response operates through the National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme (NTEP), with an emphasis on early detection, effective treatment, prevention and community participation.
Key government interventions
- Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana: Nutritional support for notified TB patients.
- Ni-kshay Mitra: Community support for patients, including nutritional assistance.
- TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan: Mobilises government institutions and communities.
- Molecular diagnostics: Tests such as CBNAAT and Truenat can help identify TB and drug resistance.
- Drug-resistant TB treatment: Shorter, all-oral regimens are improving treatment options for eligible patients.
Major challenges
1. Undernutrition increases vulnerability to TB and can complicate recovery.
2. Social stigma discourages people from seeking diagnosis.
3. Delayed detection and incomplete treatment can contribute to transmission and drug resistance.
4. Private-sector under-reporting weakens surveillance.
5. Remote and tribal communities may face barriers to timely diagnosis.
Way forward
- Strengthen active case finding and contact tracing.
- Improve nutrition and social protection for patients.
- Ensure quality-assured treatment and adherence support.
- Improve coordination between public and private healthcare providers.
- Combine medical intervention with community awareness.
Stats
- Incidence and Mortality Decline: Between 2015 and 2024, newly emerging TB cases in India dipped by 21% (against a 12% global reduction), while domestic TB mortality contracted by 28%.
- Treatment Coverage Gap: India has achieved a 92% treatment coverage rate, surpassing the global baseline of 78%.
- High Treatment Success: The national treatment success rate under the intensified TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan has risen to 90%, outpacing the global average of 88%.

Mains-ready conclusion: TB elimination requires a shift from a disease-centred approach to a people-centred public-health strategy combining early diagnosis, nutrition, social protection and community participation.
