Sea-level rise is a long-term consequence of climate change. It occurs primarily because of the thermal expansion of seawater and the melting of glaciers and ice sheets.

The UN Declaration on Sea-Level Rise provides a relevant international perspective on the vulnerability of coastal and island countries.

B. Causes of sea-level rise
Thermal expansion: As ocean water warms, it expands and occupies more volume.
Melting glaciers: Mountain glaciers contribute additional water to the oceans.
Ice-sheet loss: The melting of Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets contributes to rising sea levels.

C. Implications for India
Coastal erosion: Rising sea levels can accelerate coastal erosion and threaten settlements and infrastructure.
Agriculture: Saltwater intrusion can reduce soil fertility and affect agricultural production in coastal regions.
Groundwater: Seawater intrusion can contaminate freshwater aquifers.
Disaster risk: Coastal flooding and storm surges can pose greater risks to coastal communities.
Biodiversity: Mangroves, wetlands and other coastal ecosystems may be affected.

D. Vulnerable regions in India: Sundarbans, Coastal Odisha, Gujarat's coastal areas
Mumbai, Lakshadweep and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, degree of vulnerability differs across locations and depends on local geography, infrastructure and adaptation capacity.

E. Challenges
Inadequate coastal protection infrastructure.
Unplanned urbanisation in coastal areas.
Limited access to reliable early warning systems in some regions.
Displacement of vulnerable communities.
Financial constraints in implementing adaptation measures.

F. Way forward
Strengthen coastal zone management.
Restore mangroves and coastal wetlands.
Develop climate-resilient infrastructure.
Improve early warning systems.
Integrate sea-level projections into urban planning.
Strengthen international climate finance and technical cooperation.
