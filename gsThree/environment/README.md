# The GOBARdhan scheme was launched as a unified framework for India's Compressed Biogas sector.

The Cabinet approved it with an outlay of ₹23,731 crore, covering FY 2026–27 to FY 2035–36. The objective is to increase domestic CBG production nearly tenfold, towards about 5 MMSCMD.
What is CBG?
Compressed Biogas : Organic waste undergoes anaerobic digestion.
(Anaerobic digestion is a natural biological process where microorganisms break down organic matter in the absence of oxygen.)
Organic waste to Biogas to Purification/upgrading to Removal of CO₂, H₂S etc. to Biomethane-rich gas to Compression to Compressed Biogas

Potential sources include:
Cattle dung
Agricultural residue
Food waste
Municipal organic waste
Sewage/other biodegradable waste

Why important for India?
Energy security : Can partially substitute fossil fuels.
Waste management : Converts waste into useful energy.
Rural economy : Creates opportunities for farmers and rural entrepreneurs.
Circular economy : Waste → Energy → Fertiliser → Agriculture
Import dependence : Domestic alternative fuel can contribute to reducing dependence on imported fossil fuels.

GOBARdhan architecture
The new framework aims to bring together:
Assured offtake
Stable pricing
Capital assistance
Pipeline connectivity
Credit support
Technology development

The government has also consolidated the scheme under the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas.

# Building Water Security in Rapidly Drying India
Key data: 4% global freshwater vs 18% world popln, 11 out of 15 wtr basin face high water stress, low irrigation ie. only 20% of 72 mln hctrs of irrigated farmland 
initiative: Jal jeevan Mission, PMKSY etc

Imperative Need for Strong Water Security
• Protect urban reservoirs against seasonal shortages in Beng and Mussorie via sustainable water management.
• Reduce agricultural losses by replacing flood irrigation wid efficient methods, conservg freshwtr resources.
• Prevent aquifer depletion through regulated groundwater extraction and recharge measures.
• Measure basin-level water use to curb overextraction and improve accountability.
• Strengthen municipal finances using performance-based utilities reducing distribution losses sustainably.

Challenges
• Expand wastewater treatment infrastructure to prevent river and lake pollution.
• Develop localized climate risk data for targeted flood protection planning.
• Increase subsidies for small farmers adopting drip irrigation systems affordably.
• Reform procurement policies encouraging lowwater crops over sugarcane and paddy.
• Overall resilient water governance requires efficient infrastructure, scientific planning, community
participation, sustainable agriculture, reliable data, equitable pricing, conservation incentives, climate adaptation, institutional coordination, transparent monitoring, long-term investments benefiting people ecosystems.
• Repair aging pipelines and improve maintenance to reduce conveyance losses. 

Way Forward
• Deploy smart bulk water meters with (AI) to detect leakages and reduce distribution losses.
• Promote a circular water economy by reusing treated municipal wastewater for cooling, landscaping and industrial purposes.
• Redesign micro-irrigation subsidies using 0.4 hectares as the base unit, benefiting marginal farmers.
• Expand localized climate risk mapping to safeguard schools, electrical grids, and other critical infrastructure.
• Encourage crop diversification through stronger Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (Prime Minister’s Crop Insurance Scheme) support and faster insurance claims. 

Conclusion
• Jal Jeevan Mission has improved water access, but untreated wastewater and distribution losses remain major challenges. Combining Artificial Intelligence (AI) monitoring, wastewater reuse, and micro-irrigation can strengthen India’s longterm water security and sustainable resource management.
