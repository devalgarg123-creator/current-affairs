1) A windfall tax is a tax imposed on unusually high profits earned by businesses because of exceptional market conditions.

In the petroleum sector, governments may impose special taxes when international oil prices rise sharply and producers or exporters earn unusually high profits.

India introduced a special additional excise duty on certain petroleum products in July 2022.

- Why does the government impose windfall taxes?
    Revenue mobilisation: Allows the government to collect additional revenue when companies earn exceptional profits.
    Redistribution: Can help the government capture some of the gains arising from extraordinary market conditions.
    Domestic energy security: Can discourage excessive exports when domestic fuel supplies are under pressure.
    Fiscal flexibility: Provides an additional source of revenue during periods of exceptional commodity price increases.

- Economic implications of reducing windfall taxes
    Potential benefit	                    Potential concern
    Improves export competitiveness	        Reduces potential tax revenue
    Improves profitability for exporters	Benefits may not necessarily reach consumers
    May encourage investment	            Could reduce the government's fiscal flexibility

- Challenges
Volatility in international crude oil prices.
Balancing government revenue with industry profitability.
Ensuring domestic energy availability.
Maintaining a predictable taxation regime.

India needs a predictable taxation framework that balances fiscal requirements, energy security and investment in the petroleum sector. Any change in taxation should be assessed in light of its impact on domestic supplies, exporters and government revenue.

2) Agriculture: Minimum Support Price and Crop Diversification

The Union Cabinet approved increases in the Minimum Support Prices (MSPs) of six mandated Rabi crops for the 2027–28 marketing season on 30 September 2026.

The revised MSPs include:

Rapeseed and mustard	₹6,613/quintal
Safflower	₹7,215/quintal

The reported increase was ₹25 per quintal for wheat, ₹390 for lentil, ₹413 for rapeseed and mustard and ₹675 for safflower.

Background: Minimum Support Price
MSP is the price at which the government announces its willingness to purchase specified agricultural commodities under designated procurement arrangements.

The Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) recommends MSPs for major agricultural commodities.

The government considers these recommendations along with other relevant factors before announcing MSPs.

--Why is MSP important?
Income support: MSP can provide farmers with a price reference and, where procurement is available, a degree of price assurance.
Food security: Procurement of crops such as wheat and rice contributes to the public distribution system and buffer stocks.
Crop diversification: Differences in MSP increases can influence farmers' crop choices, although procurement, market demand and agroclimatic conditions also matter.
Price stability: Procurement operations can help manage the supply of selected agricultural commodities.

-- Diversification towards pulses and oilseeds can potentially:
Reduce pressure on water resources in some regions.
Improve soil health through appropriate crop rotations.
Reduce dependence on imported edible oils and pulses.
Increase the range of agricultural products available domestically.

However, MSP announcements alone cannot ensure diversification. Farmers also need assured markets, suitable seeds, irrigation and storage facilities.

-- Challenges
Uneven procurement across crops and states.
Regional differences in agricultural infrastructure.
Limited market access for some crops.
Rising input costs.
Water-intensive cropping patterns.
Price volatility in agricultural markets.

-- Way forward
Expand procurement and market access for pulses and oilseeds.
Promote region-specific crop diversification.
Improve irrigation efficiency.
Strengthen agricultural storage and processing infrastructure.
Improve access to market information.
Link MSP policy with sustainable agricultural practices.
